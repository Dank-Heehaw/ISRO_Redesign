NISAR carries two synthetic aperture radars (NASA's L-band and ISRO's S-band)
on one 12-metre deployable mesh reflector. Two wavelengths on the same platform
see different things: L-band penetrates vegetation to the ground beneath, S-band
resolves the canopy and surface texture above it.

Radar does not care about cloud or darkness, which is what makes a twelve-day
global repeat meaningful. Ground motion of a centimetre (a swelling volcano, a
subsiding aquifer, a creeping landslide) shows up as an interferometric fringe.

The mission entered its science phase in 2025, and S-band SAR data products are
now released publicly, including soil moisture products at 100 m resolution.
