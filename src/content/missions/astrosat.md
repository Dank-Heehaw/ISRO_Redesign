Most observatories look at the sky in one part of the spectrum. AstroSat carries
five instruments spanning far ultraviolet to hard X-rays, and can point all of
them at the same target at once. For objects that vary on timescales of seconds
(accreting black holes, magnetars, X-ray binaries), simultaneity is the whole
experiment.

Ten years on, the observatory is still operating and still producing results,
including the detection of quasi-periodic oscillations that constrain how matter
behaves in the last stable orbit before a black hole's event horizon.
