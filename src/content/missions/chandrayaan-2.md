Chandrayaan-2 launched on 22 July 2019. The Vikram lander lost contact during
its final descent on 7 September 2019, and surface operations with the Pragyan
rover never began. The orbiter, however, reached its planned lunar orbit and has
kept working well past its original one-year design life.

From a roughly 100 km circular polar orbit it continues remote sensing with
eight instruments, including Dual Frequency SAR, the Imaging IR Spectrometer,
and atmospheric sensors. Recent public releases include polar ice-related radar
products and plasma observations tied to solar storms.

Chandrayaan-3 later completed the soft-landing objective. The Chandrayaan-2
orbiter remains an active lunar asset, and its data feeds into planning for
later Indian and international Moon missions.
