From a halo orbit around L1, Aditya-L1 never has the Earth pass between it and
the Sun. That uninterrupted view is the entire point: solar storms develop over
hours, and a gap in coverage can be the gap that matters.

The Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope has captured flare development in
ultraviolet at a cadence no previous instrument achieved, including a
first-of-its-kind observation of rare plasma ejection in UV light. Alongside it,
the in-situ particle and magnetometer payloads measure the solar wind as it
arrives: the same event, imaged at the source and sampled on site.

Aditya-L1 data has already been folded into international solar storm studies,
and helped explain the unusual dawn-time geomagnetic disturbances observed during
strong storms.
