GSLV Mk II is defined by its third stage. After Russian cryogenic stages were
restricted, ISRO developed the indigenous Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS), first flown
operationally in that form in January 2014. Official figures put the vehicle at
51.73 m with an ogive fairing, about 420 t at liftoff, roughly 6 t to LEO and
2,250 kg to GTO. On 30 July 2025, GSLV-F16 placed the NASA–ISRO NISAR satellite into
a Sun-synchronous polar orbit — the vehicle's first SSPO mission.
